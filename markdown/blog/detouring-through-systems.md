# detouring through systems

Hello hello! 3 months have passed since I decided to take a detour for a bit. I have been writing it all out on my blog (stuff from the past: [living intentionally](https://vivekn.dev/blog/living-intentionally/), [a month in systems](https://vivekn.dev/blog/a-month-in-systems/), [ramblings in systems and health](https://vivekn.dev/blog/ramblings-in-systems-and-health/)).

Below are some high-level goals that I set out with:

1. Learn and do systems programming
2. Fix health and recover from surgery
3. Have a lot of fun

Here’s what happened.

### systems programming

I have been circling around this space for years now. It started with random readings in databases, debugging complex network level issues, working with [Advait](https://advaitb.com/) on a low-latency streaming pipeline at Mars, and thinking of memory safety as a mandate by God in the modern day (earlier writing on this: [going down the stack](https://vivekn.dev/blog/going-down-the-stack/)).

Many of my earlier projects were done solo. Writing a compiler is fun in isolation but the novelty dies once the project ends. Toy projects stopped fuelling my hunger. So I turned towards open-source: where I could express my ideas and code to have them reviewed and merged. A brief list of my open-source footprint is here: [oss](https://vivekn.dev/oss/). 

In August, I got interested in AI agents and the infrastructure that is needed to run them. Omnigraph is a Rust project of my interest in the memory space. They focus on creating ontologies and giving them to agents to build better knowledge graphs. The actor model also piqued my interest since it simplifies a lot of complex problems (detailed writing: [actors are cool](https://vivekn.dev/blog/actors-are-cool/)).

DHH is now bashing Rust, SlateDB is being used in more projects and Cursor’s Origin is making WAL + S3 a cool topic. Now, the conversations may go in any direction but I am happy to see that seemingly niche things of my interest are gaining momentum and becoming somewhat mainstream.

### creating value using AI

I have been helping an EV company with their tech. The great unlock here is using Codex in a way that makes my life easier. Monorepos are a debatable thing but colocated repositories opened from a parent folder with docs written throughout the way - this is a beautiful setup to empower anybody in the team. Anybody in my team can now open a fresh chat and ask the most complex and technical questions without going through me. There’s also some alpha in organizing your chats in the desktop app and coming back to them at later points in time when things have changed. Overall, I have a nice little workflow to go from idea to deployment with deep clarity along the way.
